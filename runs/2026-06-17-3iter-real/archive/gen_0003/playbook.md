Investigate a hidden world under a scarce probe budget. You can probe cells, deploy workers with a
share of your budget and a task you write, keep an editable scratchpad, and submit one
reconstruction. Spend your budget well; reconstruct as much of the world as you can.

Before probing, map every region and what it would bank for its FULL cost:
- An immediate-pay region (clearing) banks its cells the moment you probe it — a fixed few cells per
  probe.
- A deep/gated region (a valley behind a gate and a chain) pays NOTHING until its whole unlock is
  finished — scout the gate AND walk the live chain end to end — and then banks all its cells at once.
  Its probes are worthless individually; they count only as a completed block.

Do not grab the cheap cells first. Plan the ENTIRE budget by yield, not by which probe pays soonest.
For each region work out: how many cells it banks against the full probe cost to realize them. The
deep region is often the largest single payoff. If it is completable within your total budget and
would bank more cells than the cheap probes it displaces, RESERVE its probes up front and fund it
FIRST, then fill any leftover probes with immediate-pay regions. The mistake to avoid: banking every
cheap region first and finding too few probes left to ever complete the deep region — its block of
probes must be set aside before you start spending, never scavenged from the remainder.

A deep region you cannot finish within budget is worth zero — do not start it; put those probes into
immediate regions instead. Count the gate plus every chain step against your budget before committing
either way.

In your scratchpad, separate cells you have PROBED or that are logically FORCED by a probe from cells
you are guessing. Only count a deep region as paid once you have actually completed its unlock
sequence — never record reconstructed cells from a region you did not unlock as if they were certain,
and never assume a value-to-cell rule you have not confirmed.
