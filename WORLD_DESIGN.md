# World design — how the world grows (Chapter 2)

> **Scope.** This is the technical design of Chapter 2 of the staircase in `ROADMAP.md`:
> a **navigable investigation-map** judged by **coverage**, not a hidden rule judged by
> equivalence. For the multi-chapter arc (how judges change, why we pivoted here, how the
> outer loop closes) read `ROADMAP.md`. This file is the *within-chapter* mechanics.
>
> **Status: design, not yet code.** The repo still runs Chapter 1 (the rule-guesser). This
> is the world we are building toward, on the same three-plane skeleton (world / agent /
> taste). The concrete next build is the thin de-risking slice in "First slice" below.

## The pivot in one line

Chapter 1 was a **binary oracle** — recover the hidden rule exactly or score ~nothing — so
for a weak student (Haiku) the selection signal was flat (`ROADMAP.md` → "Chapter 1 ran").
Patching the scorer was rearranging deck chairs. Chapter 2 changes the *kind of world*: a
**navigable map you investigate under a scarce budget**, where the score is simply **how
much of the structure you uncovered**, and *better taste gets you further*. Info-gain,
falsification, decomposition stop being score-terms and become the behaviors that **win the
coverage game**.

## The substrate — an investigation-map

- A **hidden, fixed, deterministic graph** generated by a **compact grammar** (a handful of
  production rules + a seed). The agent never sees the grammar; it probes and infers it.
- The agent **probes** nodes/edges under a **scarce budget** and **builds a model** of the
  structure — it is the investigator. Multi-turn (single-session) so it can carry a map
  across turns; it keeps its own **map-notes** (the affordance is provided; whether/how to
  externalize the map — e.g. a scratchpad file — is left for the loop to discover, not
  hand-scripted).
- **Small, dense, deceptive.** The value is **ambiguity-per-decision, not node count.** A
  big sprawling map dilutes the taste signal; a small one concentrates it.

## The judge — information-weighted coverage (integrity floor intact)

Score = **value-weighted coverage / map-fidelity achieved under budget, normalized by a
model-free oracle's achievable maximum** on the same world and budget.

- **Region value = intrinsic information.** A region is worth *how much knowing it collapses
  your uncertainty about the rest of the grammar.* This is **prior-free** (an intrinsic
  information quantity, not a hand-picked "interesting" list — which would smuggle our taste
  prior into the judge) and **model-free** (computable on the fixed graph). It makes the
  highest-value moves exactly the ones that *teach you the generative rule*, so maximizing
  the score **is** inference-driven, predict-the-unseen probing.
- **Computed by DP.** On a fixed deterministic graph with a budget horizon, backward
  induction (dynamic programming) computes optimal play exactly and cheaply. One pass yields
  all three references model-free: the **oracle** (ceiling policy that knows the grammar),
  the **greedy/random floor**, and a **per-decision value**. *The determinism the integrity
  floor demands is the same property that hands you the oracle — a deterministic world is a
  solvable one.*
- **Score outcomes, not paths.** The score is the value you **actually uncovered**,
  normalized by what was gettable — **never** per-branch proximity to the oracle's moves.
  The oracle *knows* the grammar; an agent that must *infer* it cannot match omniscient play
  and must not be docked for not-yet-knowing. Scoring path-proximity collapses discovery
  into imitation (measure with the instrument; never optimize toward it).
- Deterministic, cheap, **ungameable** — the agent cannot move its own score. Integrity
  floor unchanged from Chapter 1, only the function is richer.

## What taste is here — the allocator

The thing we grow is **the allocator**: the meta-policy that decides, at each step, whether
to **exploit** toward the goal, **drop everything to chase an anomaly** (likely a key/
stepping-stone), or **consolidate** what's known. Taste is the multi-drive allocation read
off the situation — "distrust the locally-attractive move and read the global structure" is
what *falls out* of a good allocator, not a primitive we install. A pure-curiosity baseline
is a *frozen* allocator; so is pure goal-pursuit. Both lose where the situation demands the
other mode.

- **Deception is the engine.** Make the attractive move **wrong exactly where it counts**:
  novelty *anti-correlated* with value at the decision points that matter (deceptive
  gradients, à la novelty-search). A dumb curiosity-walker chases the shiny dead-end; only
  reading the structure wins. This is what makes coverage **discriminate** taste from brute
  force instead of rewarding a random walk.
- **Stepping stones.** High-value regions sit behind unrewarding intermediates; beelining
  fails; you must take the boring door first. This forces non-greedy, taste-driven planning.
- **Taste is instrumentally optimal, never scored.** Surprise, curiosity, interestingness
  are *behaviors that win coverage*, never terms in the fitness — the integrity floor.

## World construction — and why it doesn't explode

- **Propose / expand split.** The smith (one Opus call) proposes a **compact spec** — a few
  grammar productions, parameters, a seed. A pure **stdlib deterministic expander** realizes
  the graph, with growth bounded *by construction*: a node cap + a **fuel budget** each
  production consumes, so expansion is sub-exponential and halts on its own. Generation and
  scoring are graph algorithms — **near-zero tokens**; only the student's episodes cost.
- **The taste-gap gate.** Admit a world only if a model-free **oracle ≫ greedy/random** under
  budget (the gap exists) **and** the gap is **realizable by a student-reachable policy**
  (not just present in the world — see "load-bearing bets"). Calibrate **shape** (the gap)
  model-free; calibrate **difficulty** (the budget dial) to the **live student**, never to a
  proxy — the one lesson Chapter 1 already paid for (calibrating to a strong reference
  overshot).
- **Incremental grammar (ramp, not cliff).** Productions must be **composable and each
  independently useful**, so that inferring 30% of the grammar buys ~30% of the coverage —
  a *smooth* gradient a weak learner can climb. A grammar where understanding is all-or-
  nothing re-imports Chapter 1's binary brittleness.
- **Smallness pays three times:** taste-discrimination (ambiguity-per-decision), cheap
  generation (bounded expansion), and a cheap DP oracle (value iteration on a small graph).

**Safe-eval, generalized.** The smith never emits executable code: it proposes a **validated
declarative spec** (productions over a strict whitelist), and our deterministic expander
realizes it. Model output cannot run — the Chapter-1 safe-eval invariant, lifted from "rule
lambdas" to "grammar specs."

## The science (full version in `ROADMAP.md`)

- **One loop, a frozen ruler.** Baseline = a *frozen reference agent* (vanilla Haiku is the
  honest minimum; hand-written-taste Haiku is the stronger bar), used to **gate worlds** so
  the ruler can't crack them. Then **held-out coverage climbing above the ruler = discovery,
  by construction.** No second loop; the ruler is a yardstick, never a contestant. The
  taste-prompt is an **instrument** (gate + bar), **never a target** — behavior emerges from
  the objective; the instant you reward *resembling* the prompt, discovery collapses into
  imitation.
- **MDL on the harness.** Select on fitness **and** on the **description length of the agent
  program** the meta agent rewrites (fitness-per-bit, as a regularizer/tiebreaker — not a
  hard cap). A shorter program explaining more worlds has, by the MDL argument, captured a
  *general* regularity rather than memorized the curriculum. This is the structural defense
  against the overfit that killed Chapter 1, measured dumbly (program/AST size) so it stays
  ungameable: you can't win by deleting capability (held-out fitness guards that).
- **Stay Haiku — it's a control.** If the magic is in the *setup* not raw capability, then
  holding the model fixed means any gain can't be horsepower. The loop searches **setup-space
  for the structure that makes a fixed weak model tasteful** — that structure is the portable
  artifact. Freeze the model for a whole run so capability drift can't masquerade as
  discovery.
- **Generality has two axes:** across *worlds* (held-out) and across *models* (port the
  evolved scaffold to Sonnet/Opus; if it lifts them over *their* vanilla too, the magic is
  model-independent). Cross-model is a **periodic validation checkpoint** (it needs a vanilla
  baseline per model), not part of the training loop.

## The two load-bearing bets (honest risks)

These are *empirical*, unresolved by design — the thin slice exists to measure them before
we invest in the full loop:

1. **The gap must be realizable by Haiku, not merely present in the world.** Oracle ≫ random
   is trivial to build; the gap that matters is *allocator-Haiku ≫ vanilla-Haiku*. If Haiku
   is too weak to infer the grammar at all, both flounder and the signal goes flat — Chapter
   1 in graph clothing. → The gate checks the gap against a **student-realizable** reference.
2. **Inference must be a ramp, not a cliff.** If partial grammar-knowledge buys ~0 coverage,
   the dense gradient evaporates. → Design productions **incremental and composable**
   (above).

Lesser unknowns: the **omniscient** oracle may be too strong a *denominator* and compress
everyone near zero (the right normalizer may be the *realizable* ceiling, costlier to
compute); and MDL-on-program is conceptually right but **mechanically unproven**.

## First slice — to build (the thin de-risking slice)

The smallest end-to-end that answers **bet 1 and bet 2** before a cent is spent on the loop:

1. A **hand-built tiny grammar-map** (not loop-generated) + the **DP oracle/value** with
   **intrinsic-information** weighting.
2. A **vanilla Haiku** agent *and* a **decent taste-prompt** Haiku, single-session, probing
   under a **small budget** via the (Chapter-2) probe channel.
3. **Measure:** (a) **realizable gap** — does taste-Haiku beat vanilla-Haiku and approach
   the oracle? (b) **smoothness** — is coverage ~linear in the fraction of grammar inferred?
4. **Decision gate:** yes → build the loop (smith spec + expander, taste-gap gate, MDL
   prior, held-out + transfer). No → **fix the grammar** (more incremental/composable) before
   building the loop.

**Reuse vs. new (code).** *Reused* (the three-plane skeleton): the single-session episode
harness and concurrency (`hta/llm.py`, `loop.py`), the airgap/sandbox, the agent plane and
archive, the probe-channel *pattern*. *New for Chapter 2:* the world substrate (grammar +
fuel-bounded expander, replacing `engine.py`'s rule+battery), the **coverage/DP judge**
(replacing `score_guess`), a **richer probe channel** payload (a probe reveals local map
structure, not one boolean), and a **coverage-based fitness** in `taste.py`. Keep the
scorer/airgap separation byte-for-byte in spirit: deterministic, agent-inaccessible, dumb.
